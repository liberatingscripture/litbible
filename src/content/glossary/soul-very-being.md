*Psuche* is the life that animates a person, the self that is alive, and in the Greek of the New Testament it is not one half of a person joined to a body. The traditional rendering "soul" carries a much later idea, that a person is a material body with an immaterial soul housed inside it, and that the soul is the part that survives and matters. That idea comes from Greek philosophy by way of centuries of Christian teaching, not from these texts. When Jesus says not to be afraid of those who can destroy the body but not the *psuche*, he is not dividing a person into parts; he is saying that what someone truly is cannot be taken by violence. The word stands behind Genesis 2:7, where the human becomes a living *psuche* after God breathes into the dust, which describes a whole living creature rather than a soul placed inside one. LIT renders it as the life, the self, or the very being of a person depending on what the sentence is doing, so that a reader meets a whole person rather than a container and its contents. See also the entry for "Spirit" for the related question of what *pneuma* is doing, which is a different word and a different claim.
