*Makarios* is the word that opens each line of the Beatitudes, and it is not the same word as the one usually rendered "bless." That one is *eulogeo*, which means to speak well over someone; see the entry for "Bless" for it. The two are unrelated in origin and in meaning, and English has collapsed them into a single word, which leaves readers with the impression that Jesus is conferring something on the poor and the grieving rather than saying something about them. *Makarios* describes a condition already true of the person: they are in a good position, they have cause to be glad, whatever their circumstances look like from outside. The traditional "blessed" makes it sound like a reward being handed down, and "happy" makes it sound like a feeling, which is worse, because the people being described are grieving and hungry and harassed. LIT says they have reason for gratitude, which keeps the claim where the Greek puts it, on the situation rather than on the emotion, and which does not require someone who is grieving to also feel cheerful about it. Where the sentence needs a compact form rather than a clause, it appears as gratified.
