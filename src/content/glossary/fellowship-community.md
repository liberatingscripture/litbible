*Koinonia* is sharing, participation, partnership, and the mutual bonds that hold a group of people together. In the ancient world it covered economic ties as readily as spiritual ones, and it regularly meant the active sharing of money, goods, and responsibility. The traditional "fellowship" has drifted a long way from that. It now suggests coffee after a service, a warm feeling among people who like each other, something optional and pleasant that happens alongside the real work. The Greek is describing the real work. When Paul uses it of the collection for the poor in Jerusalem, he is talking about money moving between communities that owe each other something. When he uses it of suffering, he means people are in it together and are bearing it together. LIT renders it as community, and as sharing or participating where the sentence needs a verb, so that the word keeps its weight. Note that this is a different word from *ekklesia*, which is rendered Called Community; see the entry for "Church" for that one. *Koinonia* is what a community does and holds in common, not the assembly itself.
