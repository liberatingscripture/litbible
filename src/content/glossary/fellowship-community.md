*Koinonia* broadly refers to sharing, participation, or partnership. In ancient contexts it referred to holding or experiencing things in common (built on the root *koine*, which means 'common') and to the active sharing of resources or responsibilities. Traditionally, it has been translated a number of ways, including "fellowship," "participation," "partaking," "communion," and "sharing." The word 'fellowship' in particular has lost the meaning of the Greek, being used and understood as the optional social time church members spend together, but in the New Testament, it was the lived expression of what it meant to follow Jesus *as a community*. When Paul uses the word in reference to the collection for Jerusalem, it is an example of one of the most prominent ways the early church understood the word: material generosity extended to others, especially within and between communities. The compound *sunkoinonos* adds *sun-* ('with' or 'together') to *koinonos*, indicating a co-sharer, partner, or co-participant, and implying a deep, mutual sharing, not just of experiences but of solidarity and embodied participation. The verb refers to participating in community with people, or sharing in life together in a particularly connected way. See the entry for "Called Community" (*ekklesia*) to explore the differences. In short, *koinonia* is sharing or the concept of community in action, and *ekklesia* is the community itself, the group assembled for a purpose.
