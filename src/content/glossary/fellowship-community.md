*Koinonia* broadly denotes sharing, participation, or partnership. In ancient contexts it referred to the mutual bonds of community, economic, social, or spiritual, and to the active sharing of resources or responsibilities. Traditionally it has been translated a number of ways, including "fellowship," "participation," "partaking," "communion," and "sharing," and the first of those has drifted the furthest: "fellowship" now suggests coffee after a service, something warm and optional alongside the real work, where the Greek is describing the real work. When Paul uses the word of the collection for Jerusalem, it describes material generosity extended to others, especially within and between communities. The compound *sunkoinonos* adds *sun*, with or together, to *koinonos*, a sharer, partner, or participant, and implies a deep, mutual sharing, not just of experiences but of solidarity and embodied participation; LIT renders that one as coparticipants, which keeps the reciprocal and communal nature of both suffering and comfort. The verb refers to participating in community with people, or sharing in life together in a particularly connected way. LIT renders the family as community, sharing, and shared participation, according to what the sentence is doing. Note that this is a different word from *ekklesia*, which is rendered Called Community; see the entry for "Church" for that one. *Koinonia* is what a community does and holds in common, not the assembly itself.
