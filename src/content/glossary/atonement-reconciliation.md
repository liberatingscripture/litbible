*Hilasmos* and the words built from it are traditionally rendered "atonement" or "propitiation," and the second of those has done real damage. "Propitiation" means appeasing an angry deity, buying off wrath with a sacrifice, which is how the surrounding Greek and Roman religions understood offerings and is not how Torah understands them. The word group has to do with removing whatever stands between two parties so they can be at peace again. That is what the offerings in Torah were for, and it is worth noticing that the same word names the cover of the ark, the place where the meeting happened, which LIT renders as the reconciliation cover rather than the older "mercy seat." "Atonement" is not a bad word so much as a worn one. William Tyndale coined it in the sixteenth century as a plain English compound, at-one-ment, meaning exactly the state of being made one again, and that is precisely right; four hundred years of use have buried the sense under a theory of punishment Tyndale was not proposing. LIT says reconciliation, which is what at-one-ment meant before the word went opaque. When the tax collector in Luke 18 beats his chest, he is not asking God to be appeased but asking to be let back in.
