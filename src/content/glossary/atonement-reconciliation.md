The traditional translations are 'atonement' and 'propitiation.' *Hilasmos* refers to the sacrificial system in Torah, specifically the offerings made for sin or deviation, and its core meaning has to do with reconciliation, with removing whatever stands between two parties. In fact, the word 'atonement' was originally coined by William Tyndale in the 16th century to translate another Greek word *katallage* in Romans 5:11, which means 'reconciliation' and nothing else, and it was a compound of the English words 'at' and 'one': at-one-ment. It was always intended to indicate reconciliation, not the pardon-through-violence that 'atonement' and 'propitiation' were later understood to mean. The part of the sacrificial act that was understood to bring attention to the need for reconciliation between God and humanity, and between humans and each other, was not the killing of the bull or goat, as was the case in sacrifices made to other deities in the Ancient Near East in order to earn their favor or appease their anger. For the practices taught in Torah, it was the sprinkling of blood on the altar, and killing the animal was simply the necessary means of obtaining the blood. The blood symbolized life, and sprinkling it on the altar represented God covering over the deathly effects of sin with life, through the actions of the priest. The related word *hilasterion*, traditionally the "mercy seat," refers to anything related to making things right, reestablishing harmony, repairing relationship between two parties—especially between one party and an authority figure such as a ruler or deity—and it is translated as reconciliation cover since it is used to refer to the cover of the ark of the covenant. What Jesus accomplishes in his role as the *hilasmos* is not to appease an angry God but to provide life for those who would otherwise experience the deathly effects of harming each other. When the tax collector in Luke 18 beats his chest, he is not asking God to be appeased by a payment; he is asking God to move toward him across the distance his deviations have created.
