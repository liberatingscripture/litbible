*Pantokrator* is traditionally rendered "Almighty," which follows the Latin *omnipotens* rather than the Greek. The compound is *pas*, all, with *krateo*, which means to hold, to sustain, to keep firm. So the more literal sense is the one who holds all things together. It can carry strength, and it is not a weak word, but its center of gravity is upholding rather than dominating. That difference matters because "Almighty" in English has come to mean unlimited power to do anything, which invites the reader to picture God chiefly as the one who could crush what stands in the way, and then to work outward from that picture to everything else. In the Septuagint the word translates both *YHWH Tsevaot* and *El Shaddai*, neither of which is a claim about raw capacity. LIT says the Sustainer of All, which keeps the scope of the Greek, all things and not merely some, while naming what the holding is for.
