"Baptize" is not a translation. It is the Greek word *baptizo* written out in English letters, and it was left untranslated on purpose. The word plainly means to dip or to immerse something in a liquid, and the first translators of the Bible into English practiced infant baptism by sprinkling water over a child, so a word that said immerse would have contradicted what their churches actually did. Rather than translate it and face the problem, they carried the Greek across intact, and English readers have had a technical religious term ever since where the original audience had an ordinary word about putting something all the way under. Other languages did translate it; Dutch, for instance, renders it plainly. The act itself was a ritual immersion, an affirmation of a sacred calling and a preparation for sacred purposes, which is a claim a reader can weigh only if the word is allowed to say what it says. LIT renders it as immersing, and John accordingly as John the Immerser, so that the practice being described stays visible and the reader can decide what follows from it.
