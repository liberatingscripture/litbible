This is the word rendered "submit," "be subject to," or "be obedient to" in most translations, including in passages that have been used to demand obedience from wives, from enslaved people, and from anyone with less power in a relationship.

*Hupotasso* was a Greek military term meaning to arrange troop divisions under the command of a leader. In non-military use, the Theological Dictionary of the New Testament describes it as "a voluntary attitude of giving in, cooperating, assuming responsibility, and carrying a burden." Lexicons note that in non-hierarchical contexts it can denote voluntary alignment, cooperative responsiveness, or living out a commitment a person has already professed.

There is a detail in the military sense worth holding onto. In some contexts, such as the democracy of Athens, the one arranging the troops would take a place among the ranks once everyone was in position, as a co-participant in the battle rather than as someone standing over it.
