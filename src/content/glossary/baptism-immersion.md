"Baptism" is not a translation. It is the Greek *baptisma*, a noun built on the verb *baptizo*, written out in English letters and left untranslated. The word literally means to dip or to immerse something in a liquid, and the first translators of the Bible into English practiced infant baptism by sprinkling water over a child, so translating it as immersion would have contradicted their teaching and practice. While the literal meaning of the word does not need to prescribe what practice each modern church uses to baptize their members, it should be clear what was in view in the Bible. The original audience would have heard a word that meant something as ordinary as dip or wash, though that could extend to ceremonial or religiously prescribed washings. The practice done by John, Jesus, and their followers, was a ritual immersion, a public affirmation of a sacred calling and a preparation for sacred purposes.
