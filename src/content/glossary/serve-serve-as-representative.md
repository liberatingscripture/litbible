*Latreuo* is often translated "serve," which is correct, but it is a more specific word than just any kind of service. It means specifically to serve in a religious or governmental setting as an official who carries out ritual duties, representing the deity or leader to the people and the people to the deity; the word is used, for example, of the responsibilities of priests in representational rituals when offering sacrifices. It has to do with formal service as a member of the military or the temple staff or an attendant to a priest or king, and there is a ritual quality to it, a sense of working toward a higher purpose, and an implication of representing the one served. The related noun *latreia* refers to the service itself. The related word *leitourgia* is the concept or performance of the ritual service (and the origin of the English word 'liturgy'), and *leitourgos* refers to the person, a representative servant. Some translations use the English word 'worship,' which is a related concept, but often loses sight of the ceremonial and representational aspects that are at the center of *latreuo* and instead brings to mind the modern idea of private devotion or even corporate singing.
