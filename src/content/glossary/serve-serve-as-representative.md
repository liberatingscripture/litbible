*Latreuo* is often translated "serve," which is correct, but it is a more specific word than just any kind of service. It means specifically to serve in a religious or governmental setting as an official who carries out ritual duties, representing the deity or leader to the people and the people to the deity; the word is used, for example, of the responsibilities of priests in representational rituals when offering sacrifices. It has to do with formal service as a member of the military or the temple staff or an attendant to a priest or king, and there is a ritual quality to it, a sense of working toward a higher purpose, and an implication of representing the one served. The related noun *latreia* names that dutiful service, and the family connects with *leitourgia*, the performance of ritual service, in Philippians 2:17, and *leitourgos*, a representative servant, in Philippians 2:25. Some translations reach for "worship" instead, which pulls the word toward a modern idea of private religious devotion and loses the office entirely. It is also the wrong word to borrow, because bowing down is *proskuneo*, and the two stand side by side in Matthew 4:10 and Luke 4:8, where bowing to God and serving as God's representative are named as two different acts. LIT says serve as representative, which keeps both halves: this is real service, and it is done in someone's name.
