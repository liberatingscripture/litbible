*Latreuo* is traditionally rendered "serve," which is correct but far too broad. It is not serving in general; it is formal service performed on behalf of someone else, the way a member of the temple staff, a soldier, or an attendant to a priest or a king serves. There is a ritual quality to it and a sense of acting for a higher purpose, and above all a sense of representing the one served. Some translations reach for "worship" instead, which pulls the word toward a modern idea of religious devotion and loses the office entirely. It is also the wrong word to borrow, because "worship" belongs to *proskuneo*, which means bowing down, and the two sit side by side in Matthew 4:10 and Luke 4:8, where bowing to God and serving as God's representative are named as two different things. LIT renders it as serving as a representative, which keeps both halves: this is real service, and it is done in someone's name. That matters most in Romans 12:1, where offering the body as a living sacrifice is called a logical representative service. The claim is not that a person performs private devotion, but that they act for God in public.
