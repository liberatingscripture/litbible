*Hupokrites* is the ordinary Greek word for a stage actor, someone playing a role. In Greek plays, the actors used masks, called a *prosopon*, which is also the same word Greek uses for 'face.' The emphasis of *hupokrites* broadly is about actions performed for an audience while playing a part. While the wider Greek speaking world used it literally, Jesus takes it and uses it metaphorically for people he saw as "playing a part for an audience" in their regular lives and behaviors. The English "hypocrite" is close but not identical. It now primarily implies two-faced behavior and inconsistency, saying one thing and doing another. The Greek points at something more specific: someone who outwardly displays devotion in order to be seen doing it, rather than out of genuine faithfulness. 'Pretender' and 'pretense' both keep the sense of performance that the traditional rendering has lost.
