Traditionally, "blessed." There is no single English word that covers what is being described here. This word is *makarios*, and its Hebrew counterpart is *ashrei*. It is unrelated to *eulogeo* (both in what it means and in the linguistic root it comes from) and to *eulogeo*'s Hebrew counterpart *baruch*, the other word traditionally translated "blessed" (Matthew 21:9; 23:39). *Eulogeo* is to praise someone or to "speak well" over someone, declaring goodwill for that person, as a benediction. *Makarios* is not praise, well-wishes, or benediction. It is a declaration, a communal act of calling attention to someone having everything one could need or desire, and being positioned such that everyone would acknowledge without doubt that the person was the peak version of fortunate or well-off, a sense of absolute well-being. It was used of people with power, with victory over enemies, with wealth, and it was even what Greeks called their gods, who were beyond lack and beyond the reach of circumstances. Where this word describes people, the LIT renders it "how greatly fortunate" as the closest approximation possible. It risks making the condition sound like chance or luck, but the focus is meant to be on the supremely positive position and circumstances of a god or person.
