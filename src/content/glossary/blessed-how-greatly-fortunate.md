"Blessed" does two jobs in English that the Greek keeps apart. One of them belongs to *eulogeo*, which is to speak well over someone, a benediction; see the entry for "Bless" for that word. The other is *makarios*, whose Hebrew counterpart is *ashrei*, and the two are unrelated in root and in meaning. *Makarios* is not praise and not a wish. It is a declaration, a communal act of calling attention to someone who has everything a person could need or want and who is positioned so plainly that anyone would acknowledge it without argument. Greeks used it of the powerful, the victorious, and the wealthy, and it was what they called their own gods, who were beyond lack and beyond the reach of circumstance. That is what makes the Beatitudes startling rather than sentimental: the people being described are poor and grieving and hungry, and the declaration is made over them anyway. LIT renders it as how greatly fortunate where the subject is a person, and as lacks nothing in the three verses where the subject is God, which is the same condition in the opposite mode, necessarily rather than contingently. The related noun appears as good fortune, and the verb as declaring someone greatly fortunate.
