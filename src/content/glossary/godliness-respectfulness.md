*Eusebeia* is not connected to the word for God, which is *theos*. The traditional rendering "godliness" introduces a connection the Greek does not make. In the Greek speaking world, it was often used in religious contexts, but not exclusively. It did not mean "like God's character" or "pleasing to God" or "devout to God" or any of the other ways people understand the English word 'godliness.' The word has to do with proceeding through life in a way that exemplifies respect or reverence to others. It can be directed toward God, and often is, but it was not limited to that. It was used of the respect owed to other people, including examples of directing it toward one's parents and toward one's spouse. Several of its uses in the letters to Timothy and Titus are about how someone treats their own household. The history behind translating it "godly" does have a sense worth keeping: It was a theological connection in the understanding that treating others with respect is behaving like God. As exemplified by Jesus, being like God is not about claiming power over others but about treating people as highly valued and with dignity.
