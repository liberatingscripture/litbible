*Eusebeia* is not linguistically connected to the word for God, which is *theos*. The traditional rendering "godliness" introduces a connection the Greek does not make.

The word has to do with proceeding through life in a way that exemplifies respect or reverence. It can be directed toward God, and often is, but it was not limited to that. It was used of the respect owed to other people, including examples of directing it toward one's parents and toward one's spouse. Several of its uses in the letters to Timothy and Titus are about how someone treats their own household.

The history behind translating it "godly" does have a sense worth keeping: behaving like God. But not in the sense of claiming power over others. Just the opposite, treating people as highly valued and with dignity.
