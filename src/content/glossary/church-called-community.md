"Church" carries centuries of institutional weight that the Greek word does not. *Ekklesia* literally means "the group called out," those assembled together for a purpose, the way people would be called out for a town council meeting. By the time of the New Testament, it was a common term used in Greek speaking communities for the citizen leadership group of a town or city, much like a neighborhood association. These groups oversaw the common good and organized cooperative efforts of the residents for the care of the city and its residents. It did not have religious connotations until it was applied to the followers of Jesus who banded together to cooperate for the common good and care for their cities and fellow residents. It did not have anything to do with institutional structures, hierarchies, or authority.
